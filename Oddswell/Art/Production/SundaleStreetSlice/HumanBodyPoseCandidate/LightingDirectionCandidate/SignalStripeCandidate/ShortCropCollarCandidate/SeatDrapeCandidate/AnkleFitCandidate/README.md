# Anatomical ankle continuity candidate

This editable Blender scene starts from the [curved shoe-collar candidate](../ShoeCollarCandidate/README.md), SHA-256 `619BE913A7A7D6F4F94917BD133EBE1599E9AE320A55763B32F5B43D42FF5445`. Run `build.py` in Blender 4.5.5 with `--render` to reproduce [AnkleFit.blend](AnkleFit.blend), SHA-256 `25492A223A097BEAD790376C13D3F643BA9EB3A54729F97E51BF62DD66EBF376`, and its native Cycles review images. The original licensed street source and parent checkpoints remain unchanged.

The anatomical leg mesh previously stopped at the ankle boundary above the new shoe opening, leaving a black gap behind each leg. The 20-vertex boundary of each posed leg now continues 55 mm into its shoe. The extension widens gently into an internal foot volume, shares the leg's source skin material and UV edge colors, and is capped below the visible collar. Normal orientation and smoothing are recalculated across the connected leg mesh. The shoe upper, laces, sole and stride remain fixed.

Review at [rear close-up](Ankle_Fit_Rear_Close.png), [profile close-up](Ankle_Fit_Profile_Close.png), [full rear](Ankle_Fit_Full_Rear.png), and [gameplay](Ankle_Fit_Gameplay.png). They use 24, 24, 32, and 32 Cycles samples respectively. The ankle/collar transition is continuous in the close rear and profile studies. A cold reopen passes `verify_scene.py --draft`, and a separate topology inspection finds no open or inconsistently wound edges in the extension. This is still a static art candidate: the heel counter and sole need more construction detail, the jacket shoulders remain padded, and the face needs more natural expression. Character acceptance against the original concept and later architecture/retail/planting work remain open. There is no runtime integration claim.
