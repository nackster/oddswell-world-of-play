# Hip and seat garment continuation

`HipSeat.blend` continues the retained [articulated hand candidate](../HandArticulationCandidate/README.md). A localized native Smooth modifier softens the upper hip and seat cloth transitions while preserving the waist and leg openings and deepest inseam controls. The original mesh controls, UVs, shape keys, photographic maps, 46-bone rig and authored walking action remain unchanged. This is an editable offline visual checkpoint, not final human or runtime acceptance.

Leg assignments use the retained left/right UV panel identities, correcting 201 controls that were assigned to the opposite thigh when posed cloth crossed the body center. The existing total thigh/shin influence and pelvis/chest weights are preserved. Shared rise vertices blend both panel sides. Fourteen shorts attachments replace the inherited shorts control-cage Surface Deform binding with native Geometry Nodes sampling of the actual moving garment at fixed rest-registered cloth UVs. This prevents nearest-surface binding from switching between nearby surfaces during movement. Every evaluated UV sample is checked as valid. The patches follow the rounded seat with a fine fabric edge. Two jacket welt details that were incorrectly bound to the shorts now use jacket weights and projection onto the actual moving jacket. See `build-report.json` for every target, control count and offset.

The lower garment has a native outside-skin constraint with a 12 mm control-surface offset. Its hidden target duplicates the retained anatomical legs and removes near-horizontal closure faces; original leg geometry stays intact. The constraint is restricted to a lower-shorts vertex group. Sewn followers sample the resulting shorts surface after the panel assignments are corrected. The offset is a modeling clearance and does not imply the same measured distance at every subdivided sample.

The visible leg edges use a new restrained turned cotton band, lower roll and inward facing, registered to the same moving cloth UVs. Overlapping inherited hem followers and facings remain editable but are hidden from the render because they produced rough marks. The new edge has offsets from -0.45 to +0.50 mm and follows both leg openings.

The cold audit compares every original mesh/curve control set, mesh edges, UV layer, protected shape-key coordinates and packed image; it also compares all 46 rest matrices and six matched full-rig poses. It checks every authored integer frame for bounded attachment edge stretch and sampled distance from its assigned garment. A signed nearest-leg screen uses the rest-registered 0.60–0.70 m shorts band against skin faces, excluding artificial leg-cap faces. It reports inherited negative samples and rejects a regression deeper than one millimeter. Comparing upper shorts above the retained leg skin against its artificial closure gave misleading signed distances, so that broader screen was rejected. This is not a complete body/cloth collision proof, animated-subframe or self-intersection certification.

Review `Hips_Rear.png`, matched front/rear/profile views, `Torso_Front.png`, `Gameplay.png` and the native walking preview `HipSeatWalking.mp4`. Rendered views come from the actual native geometry and preserve the existing street composition and lighting. See `WholeFigureReview.md` for visual limitations and the next roadmap work.

Reproduce with Blender 4.5.5 LTS: run `build.py`, `bind_uv.py`, `turned_hem.py`, `audit.py`, and `render.py` sequentially with `--python-exit-code 1 --python`. Run `render.py -- --preview` for 24 numbered native frames at 12 fps. The render script uses the retained matched camera helper from `HandArticulationCandidate/render.py`.
