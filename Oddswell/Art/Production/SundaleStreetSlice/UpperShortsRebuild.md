# Upper shorts and jacket-waist rebuild

The previous editable art source is `PocketPanelCandidate/PocketPanel.blend` (SHA-256 `F4C098DFC315FF9AF49AC3F8ABE15E60D32AF28A062F7E01F5714A24B11A9052`). Its rear pockets and hems are worth retaining, but the upper shorts remain a smooth annular hip shell joined to two leg tubes. The original `develop_character_landscape.py` used a shared saddle centerline; `HumanBodyPoseCandidate/ShortsGussetCandidate/` later replaced it with a connected 39-station paired gusset. The historical 44 mm design label is not its current evaluated width: a corrected audit measures a 21.5 mm maximum span in the later `PanelBalance` source. The source-derived pelvic collision trial does not fit this posed shell closely enough to serve as a new cloth target without alignment work.

The next retained checkpoint is `WaistPatternCandidate/WaistPattern.blend`: a coupled waist/rib/jacket contour that reduces the upper shorts circumference to 0.910 m without changing the lower hip substantially. It improves the adult waist-to-hip silhouette but does not replace the annular upper shell or saddle. Use this newer scene as the source for the remaining pattern reconstruction.

The later `PanelBalanceCandidate/PanelBalance.blend` corrected front/rear depth: its measured panel-path audit (`CrewCutCandidate/PatternLayout.json`) found 0.5499 m front arc and 0.5163 m rear arc on the lower upper-shorts ring, then brought those paths to 0.5340/0.5284 m without opening the waist or moving the leg hems. The first transition trial opened dark triangles beneath the jacket band and was rejected; the retained transition ends before that band.

`GussetWidthCandidate/GussetWidth.blend` was the fit source for the later leg-axis pass. It widens the **existing** paired gusset to a measured 47.3 mm maximum and blends the adjustment through the upper legs. Front/rear close views show a broader saddle without a new hole, but the change is minor in full-body and gameplay views. The 17-ring upper shell and cloth drape still need a true front/back panel rebuild joined to the fitted pelvis and thigh support.

These trials show that displacement filtering cannot repair the current upper-shell fit. The later `LegAxisCandidate/LegAxis.blend` is the clean working scene: its lower right shorts tube follows the actual exposed thigh more closely, but its upper shell remains unchanged. The next pass must align a walking-pose pelvic/thigh support to the *actual* garment and remake the upper shell around that fit, with explicit front, back and saddle construction. Start by validating signed clearance around every upper ring and both inner thigh curves before simulating cloth. After sewing the new panels to the retained lower leg openings, hems and pockets, review front/rear/profile/full-body renders for skin gaps and self-intersection. The source-derived support and rejected drape studies are diagnostic evidence, not production assets.

A row-level check makes the alignment problem concrete. At upper-shell ring 0, 18/41 front and 8/39 rear controls penetrate the proposed support; at ring 16, 30/41 front and 12/39 rear controls penetrate. The front median signed clearance changes from +5.4 mm to -19.8 mm across those rings, while the rear median changes from +51.2 mm to +19.2 mm. The same ring contains both deep intersections and large free space. These values describe this **unfitted support**, so they are a guide to registration work, not evidence that the final garment clearance has been measured.

Orthographic front and rear Workbench overlays confirm the proposed support is grossly misregistered: its thigh mass exits the shorts on one side and its upper pelvis rises above the waist. Discard this support shape as a fitting target. A replacement must follow the *existing posed leg axes and waist elevation* before any signed-clearance number can guide panel shaping. The overlays and per-ring JSON are preserved with the rejected trials in ignored `client/OddsWell/Saved/NextTen20260912/`.

The exposed leg mesh gives a better registration reference at z = 0.62–0.69 m: its left thigh center is approximately (-0.881, -2.466) m and its right thigh center approximately (-0.621, -2.279) m. The retained garment's original hem centers were (-0.874, -2.464) m and (-0.633, -2.325) m. The right leg opening was thus about 46 mm behind its visible thigh; the accepted leg-axis correction brings its center to (-0.623, -2.291) m. A trial support built around these measured axes still intersected 455 upper-shell controls, especially at the crotch and lower hips, so it remains diagnostic. This confirms that even an aligned pose proxy needs an upper panel/saddle rebuild before cloth simulation can be trusted.

September 25 follow-up against the saved `LegAxis` scene: a second support study applied the scene's exact walking transform to a cropped MakeHuman pelvis. Its split leg transform did not produce continuous pelvic anatomy; a Workbench overlay showed the blue support leaving the shorts on one side and rising above the waist. It intersected 660/1,360 upper-shell controls (minimum signed clearance -76.9 mm). That number describes the **invalid support**, not the actual human/cloth fit. A separate 14.9 mm maximum, smoothed radial expansion guided by the earlier approximate support produced a padded rear seat and a doubled-looking right pocket edge in the native rear render. Reject both studies. More smoothing or outward ease on the existing annular ring topology is not an adequate upper-panel reconstruction.

The next modeling pass should replace the annular upper front/back shell with pattern-controlled panels shaped around a *fitted* walking pelvis and thigh support, integrating the existing gusset with those panels. Retain the two leg openings, current hems and pockets; fit their cloth panels and stitches to the new surface. Build front/rear panels, side and center construction, and the saddle as one continuous cloth pattern before adding decorative seams. Fit the ribbed band and jacket hem to the resulting waist in the same pass. Then review close front, rear, three-quarter and profile renders alongside the gameplay camera. Reject self-crossing crotch folds, exposed dark gaps, detached pockets/seams, thigh intersections, and a hem that rides up. Keep the human-realism goal active until the full figure matches the original reference visually; technical checks alone cannot accept it.

`TailoringConstructionCandidate/TailoringConstruction.blend` is the newer editable scene. It retains the same measured shorts mesh and adds surface-fitted side and fly stitch paths. Those details can follow a replacement panel if refitted, but they are not the panel geometry itself. The front/profile close renders show a modest constructed-clothing gain; the full-body and gameplay views still expose the broad upper shell and stylized human proportions.

The later `WeightBalanceCandidate/WeightBalance.blend` was the next editable scene; its shorts mesh and leg openings were unchanged from the measurements above. A further hidden pelvis fitting study blended left/right source-leg transforms continuously and limited its radius inside measured shorts cross-sections. Initial audit numbers looked better, but front/rear overlays revealed two disconnected thigh columns, an exposed top, and a protrusion near the left hem. Cropping and adding a joined pelvis volume hid the protrusions yet formed an asymmetric false central lobe; its nearest-surface audit still found 91 apparent upper-shell penetrations (minimum signed distance -31.7 mm). This study is rejected as a cloth collider. Do not infer garment fit from a row-level clearance statistic without reviewing the support surface and the whole posed figure.

The current lower-shorts checkpoint is `LongerShortsCandidate/LongerShorts.blend`, which starts from the exact `WeightBalance` scene and lowers both hem centers 50 mm. It moves the folded facings and stitch paths with the lower cloth. The full rear and gameplay views are closer to the reference's longer shorts, while the full front still reveals an overly smooth, broad upper crotch. A 4% tapered extension failed exposed-thigh clearance at 1.46 mm; the saved 2% eased extension passes at 6.69 mm. Earlier rear-seat contour and welt-pocket studies from this continuation were rejected visually: the former doubled the pocket edge and the latter looked like drawn slits on a smooth shell. Those failures reinforce the need for a true fitted upper front/back panel and saddle rebuild rather than further surface offsets or pocket decoration.
