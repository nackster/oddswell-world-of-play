# First City Concept Packages

Status: **CONCEPT B SELECTED — SUNDALE DIRECTION CANONICAL; NAME STILL WORKING**

These three Phase 1C.0 packages answer the owner's request for a first city that feels modern and distinctive without becoming plain, futuristic, luxurious, or highly fantastical. On July 20, 2026, the owner selected **Package B — Daymark / Sundale** as the beta first-city direction. Its theme, geography, visual language, atmosphere, and compact-loop intent are now canonical planning constraints under [[Design/Decisions/DEC-008 Beta First City Direction]]. **Sundale remains a working name**, and the image remains concept evidence rather than final art.

Future cities may explore much more dramatic identities, including a water-focused city and a Tartaria-inspired monumental city. Those ideas are parked after the beta first city and do not authorize current design or implementation.

See [[Visual Maps/First City Concepts|the visual comparison canvas]] and [[Development/Reports/Phase 1C0 Noncanonical First City Concept Packages|the Phase 1C.0 report]].

## Shared beta constraints

Every package preserves the same player-facing beta scope:

- One compact modern-day city that is enjoyable and understandable on foot.
- One continuous loop connecting the starter studio entrance, one job, basketball arena, physical wagering location, clothing store, furniture store, court, and streets.
- A clear arena landmark without a giant fantasy monument.
- Grounded present-day materials, technology, and scale.
- Space for shared player presence and visible clothing without designing multiplayer capacity yet.
- Environmental storytelling through work, sport, aspiration, wins, losses, and visible progression.
- Concept art is mood evidence, not a production-ready level, model, texture, license approval, or final asset.

## Package A — Mainline / Rookwell

![[90 Assets/City Concepts/Phase 1C0/rookwell-concept.png]]

**Theme:** second-shift ambition — an older working district rebuilt around basketball, apartments, shops, and evening social life.

**Geography and core loop:** mostly flat four-block rectangle. The studio row leads to a practical job entrance, passes beneath a modest rail viaduct beside the public court, continues through clothing and furniture storefronts, reaches the arena and attached sportsbook, then returns by a parallel residential street. Planning traversal target: roughly six to eight minutes before measurement.

**Skyline and architecture:** warm brick warehouse conversions, charcoal steel, weathered concrete, practical glass mid-rises, trees, and one clean arena roofline. The viaduct and arena provide two different wayfinding silhouettes.

**Minimal art direction:**

- Palette: red brick, charcoal, weathered concrete, muted teal, amber light.
- Lighting/weather: overcast early evening after light rain; reflective streets without constant gloom.
- Materials: brick, steel, concrete, glass, asphalt, painted court surfaces.
- Wayfinding: simple geometric light bands and repeated teal accents; no readable brand-dependent signs.
- Music handoff: grounded percussion, bass, and restrained late-night city energy for the owner's later music work.
- Character/item fit: earned clothing stands out clearly against muted architecture and wet neutral streets.

**Best reason to choose it:** the most grounded and graybox-efficient package while still having a recognizable identity.

**Primary risk:** it could become too dark or generically industrial. Counter with generous tree cover, warm windows, an active court, and a clean arena promenade.

## Package B — Daymark / Sundale ✅ SELECTED

![[90 Assets/City Concepts/Phase 1C0/sundale-concept.png]]

**Theme:** common ground — a warm civic neighborhood where sport, work, shopping, and social life share one shaded public center.

**Geography and core loop:** a mostly flat ring around a central commons. The starter studio and job sit on opposite sides, the clothing and furniture stores line a covered promenade, the court occupies the commons edge, and the arena/sportsbook closes the north end. Planning traversal target: roughly five to seven minutes before measurement.

**Skyline and architecture:** pale textured concrete, modest terracotta and stucco mid-rises, deep awnings, mature shade trees, brick paving, and a contemporary arena visible through the commons.

**Minimal art direction:**

- Palette: warm limestone, muted terracotta, sand concrete, sage green, restrained cobalt and burnt orange.
- Lighting/weather: late-afternoon sun with patterned shade; dry, bright, and welcoming rather than resort-like.
- Materials: stucco, textured concrete, brick pavers, glass storefronts, painted metal awnings.
- Wayfinding: the commons tree canopy, court color, and arena axis orient the player without oversized signs.
- Music handoff: light rhythmic movement, warm bass, and a social outdoor pulse for the owner's later music work.
- Character/item fit: colorful purchased outfits remain readable against pale walls and natural shade.

**Best reason to choose it:** the clearest, friendliest first-hour route and strongest immediate social atmosphere.

**Primary risk:** it could feel like a generic mixed-use development. Counter with a distinct commons shape, local material wear, repeated awning geometry, and a memorable court treatment.

## Package C — Northreach / Alderpoint

![[90 Assets/City Concepts/Phase 1C0/alderpoint-concept.png]]

**Theme:** rise to the lights — a green modern hillside district where the arena is visible above the player's modest starting block.

**Geography and core loop:** two gentle terraces connected by broad step-free switchback ramps and a shorter secondary path. The lower terrace holds the starter studio and job, the middle terrace holds the stores and public court, and the arena/sportsbook anchors the upper terrace. The return path descends through a quieter residential edge. Planning traversal target: roughly seven to nine minutes before measurement.

**Skyline and architecture:** dark brick, exposed concrete, glass mid-rises, planted retaining walls, tall native trees, and an arena integrated into the upper slope rather than sitting on a pedestal.

**Minimal art direction:**

- Palette: slate blue, dark brick, moss and evergreen foliage, weathered concrete, amber windows, restrained yellow wayfinding.
- Lighting/weather: cool blue hour after a cloudy day; warm pools of light keep the district inviting.
- Materials: concrete, dark brick, galvanized metal, glass, wet stone, planted walls.
- Wayfinding: arena light above, court below, and repeated yellow route accents on every accessible connection.
- Music handoff: atmospheric low-end, crisp percussion, and a gradual sense of ascent for the owner's later music work.
- Character/item fit: off-white starter clothing and later bright outfits read strongly against the darker green-and-slate environment.

**Best reason to choose it:** the most memorable skyline and strongest visual metaphor for progression.

**Primary risk:** grade changes increase navigation, collision, camera, and accessibility work. Counter with only two terraces, gentle ramps, short sightlines, and a flat alternate return path.

## Owner decision outcome

The owner selected Package B on July 20, 2026. Phase 1C.1 subsequently froze Sundale's minimal art direction and `800 m` commons-ring loop, and Phase 1C.2 proved that primitive route in a packaged build. See [[Design/Sundale Art Direction and Core Loop]] and [[Development/Reports/Phase 1C2 Primitive Sundale Graybox and Packaged Route Measurement]]. The working city name may still be changed without reopening the selected city direction.
